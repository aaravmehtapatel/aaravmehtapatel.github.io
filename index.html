PARS

Portfolio aggregation and reconciliation system, architect and sole developer

A full-stack tool that reads a client’s custodian PDF statements, checks that every holding and total ties out, and exports an analyst-ready Excel workbook, Envestnet and Google Sheets files, and a PowerPoint review deck. It replaces the four-to-five-hour manual statement cycle advisors repeat for every client.
  • Proposed the idea to the firm’s managing director while interning at Planning Alliance, then designed and built the whole system myself.
  • AI extraction feeds a deterministic reconciliation engine that foots and cross-foots every statement and blocks export when a number doesn’t tie.
  • A security master learns CUSIPs across custodians and ranks each by where it came from, so an analyst’s confirmation is never overwritten by a guess.
  • Analytics cover asset allocation, fund look-through, bond analytics, equity insights, unrealized gains and tax-loss harvesting, asset location, fee audits, and model-portfolio proposals.
  • Every exported figure is marked as sourced, derived, assumed, or pulled live from Bloomberg. The codebase has more than 470 automated tests.

Household reconciliation engine

Open-source Python library

A from-scratch rewrite of the core ideas behind PARS: reconciling one family’s holdings across several custodians, when the same bond can be printed four different ways.
  • Normalizes security names so different spellings of one bond collapse together, while refusing to merge names that only probably match.
  • Ranks identifiers by source, analyst over statement over model, and never saves a model’s guess.
  • Separates blocking breaks from items that need review. The demo plants three errors in a four-custodian household and catches all three.
  • Uses exact decimal money throughout, with 30 tests and no outside dependencies.

Gnosis

World Cup 2026 prediction model

Python package, sports analytics

A match-prediction model for the 2026 World Cup that turns two teams’ strength into a probability for every possible scoreline, with a live mode that re-prices a match as it unfolds and a Monte Carlo simulator that plays out groups and knockouts.
  • Before kickoff, on real June 2026 fixtures, its win, draw, and loss probabilities landed within 2 to 3 percentage points of sportsbook odds once the bookmaker’s margin was removed. For Brazil vs Scotland it gave 67/21/12; the market implied about 70/19/12.
  • In walk-forward tests on simulated seasons, it scored a ranked probability score of 0.208, against 0.222 for an Elo baseline and 0.235 for base rates, with a calibration error of 0.005, and recovered the true team strengths at a 0.97 correlation.
  • A live mode updates win probabilities as a match unfolds. In the Argentina vs Egypt round of 16, it gave Argentina a 67% chance while they trailed 2–0 in the 70th minute, when sportsbooks implied only about 8–9%. Argentina scored three late goals and won 3–2 in stoppage time.

How it works

  1. Goals follow a Poisson distribution. Each team’s goals in a match are modeled as Poisson with a rate set by its attack, the opponent’s defence, and home advantage.

    λhome = exp(attackhome − defenceaway + home),   P(k goals) = λke−λ / k!

  2. Strengths are fitted by maximum likelihood. Every team’s attack and defence are chosen to make past results as likely as possible, with older matches down-weighted by exponential time decay.

  3. The Dixon-Coles correction fixes low scores. Independent Poisson models misprice 0–0, 1–0, 0–1 and 1–1, so a dependence parameter ρ adjusts just those four cells.

  4. A score grid gives every outcome. Multiplying the two distributions gives the probability of each scoreline up to 12–12. The diagonal sums to the draw probability, one side to a home win, the other to an away win. The win margin is checked against the Skellam distribution, the exact law for the difference of two Poissons.

  5. Live games are anchored to Elo ratings. The rating gap Δ gives an expected result, and the model solves for the goal margin D that reproduces it, holding total goals at 2.6. Host nations get +100 Elo.

    E = 1 / (1 + 10−Δ/400),   λhome = (2.6 + D) / 2,   λaway = (2.6 − D) / 2

  6. Simulation adds the uncertainty. Each match is confirmed with 50,000 Monte Carlo runs, group standings with 300,000, and 20,000 random perturbations of the ratings produce 90% intervals around every probability.

  7. Forecasts are graded, not just made. The ranked probability score rewards putting probability close to the real result; expected calibration error checks that 30% events happen about 30% of the time; Kelly sizing shows what a bet would be worth if the edge were real.

    RPS = ½ Σ (cumulative forecast − cumulative outcome)²,   Kelly f* = (bp − q) / b

S&P 500 multi-factor index model

Excel and Bloomberg, built during an internship

A Bloomberg-linked workbook that reverse-engineers the S&P 500 Quality, Value and Momentum index to recover the constituent weights Bloomberg doesn’t publish, and explains why each stock was added, dropped, or kept at the June 2026 rebalance.
  • Rebuilds S&P’s methodology in Excel: rank-based normal-score factors, the 80/120 buffer that protects incumbents, and a water-fill solver for weight caps.
  • Found and fixed a ranking bug that could silently corrupt the top-100 selection, replacing a COUNTIF comparison with an exact SUMPRODUCT one.
  • Matched the momentum window, quality composite, and value ratios to the published index rules.
